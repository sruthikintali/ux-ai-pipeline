DESIGNING THE PIPELINE

Turning a UX process into an executable system.

Process

UNDERSTAND → DEFINE → STRATEGIZE → DESIGN → REALIZE

Start with the UX process as the backbone.

Skills

Make UX activities executable.

Each Skill owns a specific UX activity and its implementation.

Agents

Assign specialised responsibility.

Agents execute related Skills and own execution responsibility.

Phases

Create controlled execution boundaries.

Five Phases organise the pipeline from project understanding to prototype realization.

Human Control

Keep critical decisions human.

Human Decision: Chooses between valid directions.
Human Gate: Approves whether completed work may progress.

Orchestrator

Coordinate the system.

The Orchestrator routes execution across Phases, Agents, Skills and human controls.

What began as a UX process is now an orchestrated, human-controlled design pipeline.

HUMAN DECISION

WHAT IT DOES: Selects between multiple valid directions.

WHY IT EXISTS: Some choices are product judgement rather than quality checks and should not be autonomously made by the AI system.

CURRENT INSTANCE: Concept Selection between S07 and S08.

IMPACT: Prevents the pipeline from silently choosing product direction.

HUMAN GATES (G01–G05)

WHAT THEY DO: Stop automatic progression and require explicit human review.

WHY THEY EXIST: AI may execute UX work, but humans remain accountable for whether the work is acceptable to continue.

AVAILABLE ACTIONS: APPROVE · REVISE · REJECT

G01 — Project Understanding Review: Reviews P01 before research and definition continue.
G02 — Research & Definition Review: Reviews personas, pain points and problem definition before strategy begins.
G03 — Solution Strategy Review: Reviews the selected concept and information architecture before experience design begins.
G04 — Experience Design Review: Reviews flows, features and experience blueprint before realization begins.
G05 — Experience Realization Review: Reviews the interactive prototype as the final Design Pipeline artifact.
A Gate answers: “May this work proceed?”
A Human Decision answers: “Which valid direction should proceed?”
SYSTEM ORCHESTRATOR

WHAT IT DOES: Routes execution through the pipeline.

WHY IT EXISTS: Skills, Agents, Phases and Gates define responsibilities, but something still needs to determine what executes next.

RESPONSIBILITIES:

  • Starts the appropriate Phase
  • Routes work to the responsible Agent
  • Ensures Skills execute in the intended sequence
  • Carries outputs forward as inputs
  • Stops at Human Decisions
  • Stops at Human Gates
  • Routes REVISE outcomes back into the appropriate work
  • Continues only after human approval

The Orchestrator does NOT perform UX work itself. It coordinates the system.
